# Search record for formalization precedents

Search date: 28 September 2026 UTC, 27 September Pacific time. Purpose: determine whether proof assistants have previously been used for social choice or game theory, and screen for direct precedents for the proposed sociology and psychology work.

The query record is in formalization-search-log.json. Both general web engines were used. Primary project pages, author papers, the Archive of Formal Proofs, and official product documentation were preferred for retained claims. Broad searches produced irrelevant uses of the word Lean; these were excluded. This was a targeted discovery pass, not a preregistered systematic search with a complete screening ledger.

Decisive prior examples were Nipkow's Isabelle/HOL social-choice development, Holliday, Norman, and Pacuit's Lean voting paper, and public Lean social-choice and game-theory repositories. These are sufficient to reject a claim that the broad activity is new. Repository READMEs establish that a project exists and state its advertised scope; they do not establish that every advertised result has been independently checked in this review.

Queries combining Durkheim with Lean, formalization, or theorem prover did not yield a clearly matching formal development in the reviewed results. Psychology queries also did not recover the user's original corpus or establish a complete field inventory. Neither absence is evidence of priority. A stronger novelty review should search author bibliographies, formal-library inventories, mathematical sociology, economic theory, psychometrics, and relevant workshop proceedings; ask domain experts about differently named or unpublished work; and compare exact statements and assumptions.

The Durkheim text used is an attributed university-hosted excerpt of the Simpson translation, not a complete-book reading. The proposed task model is the project's interpretation and remains unimplemented. The paper and model should retain that distinction during future development.
