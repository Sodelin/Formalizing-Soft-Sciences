# Proposed mechanism study

Status: design proposal, not registered or conducted. No participants have been recruited, data collected, effect sizes fabricated, or ethical approval claimed.

The first study asks whether understanding a shared cultural code affects costly cooperation separately from payoff interdependence and institutional reliability. It should use an experimentally learned microculture, avoiding the inference that ethnic identity can be assigned like a treatment.

Use a randomized 2 × 2 × 2 factorial design. Factor A provides either shared symbolic training with a partner or matched-duration unrelated training; both conditions receive equal attention and comprehension checks. Factor B varies a precisely disclosed payoff schedule so mutual contribution either strongly or weakly aligns participants' material interests. Factor C provides either a reliable third-party guarantee or the same game without the guarantee. The guarantee’s payments and funding must be explicit. All eight cells receive identical base compensation and clear task instructions. Randomize at dyad or session level as required to prevent information spillover; analyze at that assignment level.

The main outcome is each participant's contribution from a fixed token endowment in the first incentivized interaction, averaged within assignment unit where necessary. Before seeing outcomes, specify the meaningful difference in tokens, the primary factorial contrasts, and one theoretically central interaction: shared symbolic training × institutional guarantee. Other interactions are exploratory unless powered and designated in advance. Estimate intention-to-treat effects with confidence intervals and randomization-consistent or clustered inference. Report the payoff manipulation as a change in incentives, not an isolated change in identity.

Measure perceived similarity, symbolic comprehension, affiliation, expected reciprocity, and perceived institutional reliability separately. Elicit expectations before the first choice, using the same procedure in every arm; assess in a pilot whether elicitation itself changes behavior. These variables are potential mediators or manipulation checks. Do not adjust for post-treatment measures in the primary total-effect model. A mediation analysis requires additional identifying assumptions and should remain secondary.

Secondary outcomes are willingness to choose the same partner again, actual follow-through, distribution of earnings, perceived voice, and behavior with a new partner after a prespecified delay. A one-week follow-up is a feasible initial target, not evidence of long-term durability. Estimate transfer separately from repeat-partner behavior. Record loss to follow-up by arm and give missing-data sensitivity analyses. Avoid conditioning the analysis on successful training if training is part of assignment.

Determine sample size prospectively using a blinded pilot variance estimate or a credible external estimate, a justified smallest meaningful effect, anticipated attrition, clustering, and the intended multiplicity adjustment. Calculate power for the central interaction as well as main effects. Do not select a convenient sample size and call it adequately powered. Publish the design, analysis code, stopping rule, and outcome definitions before collecting confirmatory data.

A second study can test significant-other resemblance. Obtain informed consent for participants to supply nonidentifying personality descriptors of an important person. Manipulate a fictional partner profile’s resemblance while holding stated reliability and task incentives constant. Distinguish positive, negative, and ambivalent relationship valence. No facial resemblance inference, private third-party data, or demographic essentialism is needed. Preregister whether familiarity and expected reciprocity mediate different responses. This study should remain separate initially so a complex factorial design does not obscure mechanisms.

Generalization requires organizational field work. Candidate units include volunteer associations or food cooperatives; compare representation rules and repeated shared tasks using a feasible randomized rollout or a defensible quasi-experiment. Measure who leaves, who receives benefits, and whose complaints are resolved. A lab token effect cannot establish constitutional or migration policy. Any real study requires appropriate ethics review, local consultation, consent, participant protection, and a funded implementation plan.
